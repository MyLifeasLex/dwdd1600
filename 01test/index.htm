Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the responsible, safe, and ethical use of technology, the internet, and digital devices. It involves respecting others online, protecting personal information, and using digital resources appropriately.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law refers to the rules and regulations that govern the use of technology and the internet. It includes issues such as copyright, plagiarism, cyberbullying, hacking, and online privacy.

What is AUP?

AUP (Acceptable Use Policy) is a set of rules that explains how users are allowed to use a school's, organization's, or company's technology and internet resources. It outlines acceptable behavior and the consequences for misuse.

What is plagiarism?

Plagiarism is the act of using someone else's words, ideas, images, or work and presenting them as your own without giving proper credit.

It can include copying text, paraphrasing without citation, or using another person's work without permission. Plagiarism is considered unethical and can lead to academic, professional, or legal consequences.

What is academic dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any action that gives a student an unfair academic advantage or misrepresents their work.

Examples include cheating on tests, plagiarism, copying assignments, or using unauthorized materials during exams.

What are the rules for AI Generated Content?

Rules for AI-generated content vary by school or organization, but generally require users to be honest about using AI, follow assignment guidelines, and avoid presenting AI-generated work as entirely their own.

Users should verify the accuracy of AI-generated information and properly cite or disclose AI use when required.
